# Estimator computation: client-side, packages in Sanity, methodology in code

Status: accepted

## Decision

The Solar Rooftop Estimator runs its math **client-side** in a `'use client'` component. It is fed two sources of numbers, deliberately kept separate:

- **Business numbers in Sanity** — a `solarPackage` document type (`sizeKw`, `phase`, `segment`, `price`, `active`, `order`) plus the electricity tariff. Employees maintain these in Studio with no deploy, exactly like `trainingSession` and `installer`.
- **Methodology numbers in code** — `lib/estimator/constants.ts` holds kWh-per-kWp yield, grid CO₂ / water / tree factors, usable-area factor, m²-per-kW, system lifetime, and the **verdict thresholds** (good/marginal payback years, minimum viable kW). Each is commented with its source and changed only via reviewed PR.

The page (a server component) fetches packages from Sanity and imports the code constants, then hands both to the client estimator as props. All recomputation (slider, roof redraw) happens locally with no network round-trip.

## Why

- **Instant interactivity.** The day/night slider and roof-redraw must recompute with zero latency; a server route per keystroke would be sluggish and pointless for simple arithmetic.
- **The split protects the claim.** The verdict ("don't install") is a statement WS Energy makes to a customer. Prices and tariffs genuinely change and are business-owned, so they belong in Sanity. But the emission factor and the payback thresholds that decide the verdict should be version-controlled, reviewed, and auditable — not silently editable by a marketing login. This is why some constants are intentionally *not* in Sanity, against the otherwise-uniform "everything in Sanity" pattern.

## Consequences

- A future engineer will find methodology constants in code while everything else is in Sanity. That asymmetry is deliberate (see above), not an oversight.
- AGENTS.md's "don't change Sanity schemas" rule is scoped to the brand redesign; adding `solarPackage` is a new-feature schema addition, consistent with the Training/Installer precedent.

## Amendment — PEA parity & phase-only model (2026-06-26)

The engine was retuned to **faithfully replicate PEA's public Solar Calculator** (peasolar.pea.co.th), reverse-engineered from four benchmark runs the team captured (pinned in `engine.test.ts`). PEA's outputs are perfectly linear in system size; the code constants are now **calibrated** to reproduce them:

- **savings ฿480/kWp·month** (yield 1,371.43 kWh/kWp × ฿4.2 tariff), **CO₂ 498.8 kg/kWp·yr**, **fuel oil ~219.95 L/kWp·yr**, **trees ≈8.05/kWp (10-yr equivalent)**.
- **Two yields, on purpose.** PEA *sizes* on a more optimistic production assumption than it *reports* savings with — at a tier boundary it lands one tier smaller. We model this with a separate `PV_SIZING_YIELD` (1,400 kWh/kWp, the national-average figure) used only to compute the ideal kWp, while `PV_SPECIFIC_YIELD` (1,371.43) drives production/savings/CO₂/etc. This reproduces every benchmark *and* boundary cases (e.g. ฿19,232 @ 50% 3-phase → 15 kW, not 20 — confirmed against PEA). "Size generously, promise conservatively." If a future boundary case diverges, nudge `PV_SIZING_YIELD`.
- The **water-saved metric was replaced by fuel oil** — PEA displays fuel oil, not water.
- The **grid CO₂ factor was lowered to 0.3637** (from the official TGO 0.475) *specifically to match PEA*. Replication now takes priority over our own sourcing for this number; the divergence is intentional and documented in `constants.ts`.
- The **customer-segment input was dropped**: PEA's calculator is driven purely by connection **phase**. Single-phase caps at 5 kWp, three-phase at 20 kWp (confirmed by the benchmarks). `EstimatorInput`/`EstimatePayload`/the RFQ hand-off no longer carry `segment`; project type is inferred from system size, and estimator leads no longer force a company field.
- The **package ladder** mirrors PEA's tiers, **all six PEA-confirmed**: 1-phase 3 kW=฿118,500, 5 kW=฿139,000; 3-phase 5 kW=฿163,900, 10 kW=฿214,400, 15 kW=฿316,500, 20 kW=฿388,200. The 3-phase 5 kW price (฿163,900, higher than the 1-phase 5 kW because of the three-phase inverter) was pinned by the ฿40,000 / 60 m² roof-capped benchmark. Prices stay flagged as PEA-benchmark figures (`seed-solar-packages.mjs`) until the team enters real WS Energy turn-key prices in Studio. The `solarPackage.segment` field remains in the schema but is ignored by the (phase-driven) engine.
- **Sizing rounds to the NEAREST tier (not snap-down, not a floor).** The ideal size = daytime kWh ÷ a design yield, mapped to the closest available package. A roof-sweep and small-bill sweep pinned this decisively: ฿6,000 3-phase / 50% → 5 kW but ฿9,000 → 10 (the 5→10 flip sits *between* them = rounding), while ฿14,500 → 15 and ฿15,000 → 15. Snap-down is mathematically impossible here (฿9,000's ideal ~8 needing 10 while ฿15,000@60%'s ideal ~16 needing 15 cannot both hold under one snap-down yield); round-up is ruled out by ฿8,000 → 5 (not 10). The **design yield is 1,600 kWh/kWp/yr**, the centre of the (1,570, 1,657) window the confirmed 3-phase bills bracket. The 3-phase 5 kW tier is therefore a normal bill-reachable tier (small three-phase bills land on it), *and* the roof-cap fallback. (This supersedes two earlier notes in this same amendment — one claiming PEA had no 3-phase 5 kW tier, one claiming a 10 kWp three-phase floor with snap-down; both were artifacts of an incomplete benchmark set. The load-driven `MIN_RECOMMENDED_KWP` floor they introduced was removed.)
- **Self-consumption cap confirmed by the load-capped benchmark.** PEA's outputs are linear in kW only while the system is the binding constraint. The ฿9,000 3-phase / 50% case sizes to 10 kW but its 10 kW production (13,714 kWh) exceeds the daytime load (12,857 kWh), so savings/CO₂/fuel/trees all track the **daytime load**, not production: monthly savings = exactly half the bill (฿4,500), CO₂ 4,676 kg, fuel 2,062 L, 76 trees — all reproduced exactly. The engine already modelled this (`annualOffsetKwh = min(production, daytime kWh)`); the benchmark just pins the load-capped side. Separately, the tree constant was nudged **619.6 → 618.5 kg/tree** (the window that rounds *every* benchmark's tree count to PEA's displayed integer is [617.7, 619.4]; 619.6 sat just outside it and was off-by-one on this case).
- **Roof model retuned and PINNED by a sweep — PEA is tighter than the "75% rule".** At a fixed ฿40,000 3-phase bill (so the roof, not load, binds), PEA returns 60 m² → 5 kW, 75 m² → 10, 120 m² → 15, 180 m² → 20. These land exactly on an effective **~7.5 m²/kWp gross** (vs the ~6 the "75% rule" gives), implemented by lowering `USABLE_ROOF_FRACTION` 0.75 → **0.6** (0.6 · area / 4.5 = area / 7.5). The sweep bounds the effective figure to (6, 7.5]; 0.6 sits at the top, where 75 m² is exactly the 10 kWp threshold PEA returns. Roof cap = largest tier ≤ this (a hard limit, never rounded up past the roof); a system is `roofLimited` when the roof forces a smaller tier than the phase-capped load pick would. The fuel-oil factor was also nudged 0.16038→0.16039 once the 10 kW benchmark tightened it.
- **Verdict policy → lean-to-recommend (sales tool).** The original 3-tier "install / consider / not yet" gate was relaxed: this is a lead-gen tool and the business wants to *sell*, so whenever a system fits and saves money we render **"Recommended"** regardless of payback length or how small the system is. The payback bands now only select honest sub-copy, not the verdict. "Not yet" (a soft "let's talk") survives **only** for the genuinely impossible cases: no package fits the roof/phase, or there's no daytime load to offset (no/zero bill, all-night usage). This supersedes the grilling-session 3-tier decision below.
